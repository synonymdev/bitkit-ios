# Profile

`delete-profile.xml` verifies visible progress and completion when deleting a disposable profile. Use a test identity that may be deleted.

`profile-removal-recovery.xml` covers original-owner confirmation, uncertain deletion, strict local cleanup and restart recovery. Use separately provisioned, unfunded identities and controlled operation/storage faults. Successful deletion still withdraws public/private endpoints, uses the confirmed native profile revision and finishes required local cleanup before leaving Profile. Partial or unknown results must never appear as success.

After a destructive attempt starts, its durable local obligation keeps authenticated work, automatic endpoint publication and identity replacement paused until explicit local Disconnect completes. Cancellation, leaving the screen, foregrounding, reconnecting and restart do not replay remote deletion. A pending profile cleanup must keep ordinary receive/accounting available; a pending wallet wipe must keep wallet startup blocked. Required credential erasure must acknowledge before consumed-payment-list safety state is cleared, and the exact obligation is removed last. Hold each phase to verify stale completions cannot act on another owner, including a later session for the same key.

The iOS recovery journey deliberately differs from Android's Review UI: this pinned native contact read can reconcile remote writes. This patch therefore offers local Disconnect rather than claiming a read-only remote retry. Native observation and conditional contact deletion across edits, re-adds and ABA remain separate work. Local Disconnect never asserts that remote deletion succeeded and must not erase Ring-owned credentials.

The automatic-publication timing checks below use a temporary in-process admission pause **without a durable removal obligation**. They verify ordinary recovery after that pause, not resumption after uncertain destructive cleanup. For a real failed removal, expect the durable recovery screen and no publication until acknowledged local completion and a subsequent explicit sign-in.

Hold invoice preparation or the final identity lookup before publication, begin deletion, then release it. New public/private endpoint rotation must stay deferred while matching paid-invoice invalidation and receipt attribution still complete. Already-admitted writes may finish, but an older publication must not acknowledge a newer paid-invoice invalidation. These checks require controlled payment events and SDK-admission gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

Hold endpoint preparation before publication admission, or queue a public publication behind SDK work. Begin deletion, complete endpoint removal while session revocation remains held, then release the queued preparation/publication. The removed endpoints must stay absent. Separately, let a publication enter its SDK write before deletion: cleanup must wait for it and remove its endpoints afterward, even if its caller is cancelled. This requires controlled operation gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

On iOS, public publication and endpoint removal share an endpoint lock. A publication already holding that lock while waiting for SDK work must finish or stop before removal can complete. Hold preparation before that lock to exercise removal before a stale publication is released. Explicit manual sharing settings are outside these automatic-publication checks.

Hold initial session restoration with public contact payments enabled, turn contact payments off, then release restoration. No automatic endpoint preparation or publication may resume, and pending cleanup must remain available. Repeat with publication waiting for SDK admission. If a write was already admitted, let it finish and verify it cannot clear the cleanup requested by disabling sharing. These checks require restoration and SDK-operation gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

While deletion is held, make a Lightning channel usable or ready, then release the temporary admission pause while retaining the same identity. Public endpoint preparation must refresh the invoice rather than reuse its old route hints, even if an older invoice creation finishes after the channel change. A skipped or failed publication must leave reconciliation pending; an older admitted write must not acknowledge a newer channel refresh. Sign-out, identity replacement, cancellation or disabled sharing must not let older preparation refill the invalidated cache. Also hold an older public endpoint snapshot before publication, pay its invoice and publish the replacement, then release the older snapshot. The replacement must become pending again and be republished by normal reconciliation. These checks require channel, failure and publication gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

Repeat the held snapshot check while enabling contact payments and while a failed enable restores the previous sharing preference. A late stale publication must remain pending for repair; a successful current publication must acknowledge earlier pending work. Explicit disabling must still remove endpoints and retain failed cleanup for retry.

While enabling contact payments, invalidate the public invoice during address preparation or invoice creation without changing the session. Enabling must re-prepare once and keep sharing enabled after publication. Repeated invalidation must stop with a publication failure, while creation or decoding errors must propagate without retry. Cancellation, identity changes, disabled sharing or UI, and active deletion must not admit replacement preparation. These checks require controlled preparation and invalidation gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

On iOS, these channel-refresh checks cover public endpoints. Private endpoint preparation retains its existing cleanup and reconciliation behavior.

Hold initial session restoration before sign-in completes, make a Lightning channel usable or ready, then release restoration while public publication remains blocked. Restore readiness or connectivity without another channel event. Normal public maintenance must retain the forced invoice refresh for the restored identity, including after a deferred or failed restoration and a skipped or failed publication. Work retained for the stored identity or an existing signed-in session must not transfer to a replacement identity or a new session for the same key. These checks require restoration, channel and publication gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

`unlink-ring-pubky.xml` verifies that a pubky adopted from Pubky Ring shows "Unlink pubky" instead of "Delete Profile" in Edit Profile, returns to profile onboarding after unlinking, and stays available in Pubky Ring. It needs Pubky Ring on the same simulator, signed by the same Apple team, which is not a listed capability. Use a disposable Ring identity; a profile created in Bitkit keeps "Delete Profile", covered by `delete-profile.xml`.

`signup-create-profile.xml` verifies that signup through staging.pubky.app can finish profile creation without a false Profile Error toast. Use a fresh, unfunded test wallet and keep the app process alive. The profile must use the same public key as the authorized signup.

Delayed or failed session restoration requires network fault injection, which is not a journey-runner capability; see the PR manual checks.

For a saved identity, inject a temporary import failure (`concurrent_update`, `shared_state_busy`, or a transport failure) during cold start. Verify that no "Session expired" toast appears, the saved credentials remain, and recovery succeeds after the temporary failure ends. A genuinely invalid session must still produce the existing expiry feedback.

For the reported background-resume case, verify returning directly to Profile with the process still alive, then repeat after the OS recreates the process. A delay in session recovery must keep the saved identity on the profile loading/retry screen rather than show profile onboarding.

On iOS, open a saved contact's link while a stored identity's session restoration is held, deferred or failed. The link must stay pending without opening Add Contact, reading private contacts or showing a QR error. Restore the session and verify that the retained link opens the saved contact once contacts are ready, without opening the link again. With no stored identity, including after successful profile deletion, the public contact must still open read-only without granting authentication. A cached profile name alone is not a stored identity. These checks require restoration and network-failure gates beyond the journey runner capabilities.

`create-profile.xml` records the name-only saving interval and checks the saved name after reopening and relaunching. Use a fresh unfunded identity per run and record the exact app/SDK revisions and network. With controlled network fault injection, block direct UDP while keeping the configured HTTP relays and homeserver reachable; saving should still complete. Reject the required profile write separately and verify saving reports failure instead of navigating to Pay Contacts. Fault injection is a manual check beyond journey-runner capabilities.

On iOS, the restart check waits for the full profile (`ProfileViewName`). A matching `ProfileCachedName` during loading does not complete that check. Record an unfinished load separately from a loaded profile with a missing or incorrect name.
